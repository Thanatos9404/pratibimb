# Model card — Pratibimb T2D digital twin

> **Not a medical device.** Research proof of concept built and trained on **synthetic** patients only.
> Sections 1–5 come from `reports/metrics.json` / `reports/cohort_validation.json` generated by
> `pratibimb build --profile full`. They describe held-out synthetic patients generated by a
> different, richer simulator than the twin, so they are **not** evidence of performance on real people.
> Section 5b is a separate external check on **real**, open, de-identified CGM data
> (`reports/real_validation.json`, [REAL_DATA.md](REAL_DATA.md)).

## 1. What it is

A per-patient digital twin of glucose–insulin physiology for a person with Type 2 Diabetes that

1. is **personalised from the EHR** (HbA1c, fasting glucose, TyG index, waist/height, BMI, diabetes
   duration, C-peptide when present, eGFR, medications, TCF7L2 genotype as a weak prior);
2. is **kept in sync** with CGM by an Ensemble Kalman Filter that re-estimates hidden physiology
   (insulin sensitivity, beta-cell responsiveness, hepatic glucose output, glucose effectiveness, meal
   absorption speed, dawn effect) every 5 minutes, using wearable signals (steps, sleep, HR/HRV) and
   logged meals/doses as inputs;
3. **forecasts** glucose 4 h ahead as an ensemble cone (with a habitual-meal prior);
4. feeds a **gradient-boosted risk layer** (LightGBM + isotonic calibration) that predicts level-2
   hyperglycaemia within 2 h, hypoglycaemia within 1 h and nocturnal hypoglycaemia tonight, with
   TreeSHAP drivers mapped to clinician-readable text and hysteresis-controlled alerts;
5. answers **what-if** questions (Indian meal swaps, post-meal walks, skipped/shifted doses, a short
   night) on this patient's twin.

Intended use: a conceptual clinician console for triage and counselling in a hackathon demonstration.
Out of scope: any clinical decision, dosing, real patient data, paediatric/T1D/pregnancy use.

## 2. Twin model (what the twin actually runs)

States: plasma glucose `G` (mg/dL), remote insulin action `X` (1/min), plasma insulin above basal `I`
(µU/mL), gut compartments `D1, D2` (mg), interstitial glucose `Gi` (mg/dL), carried gut time-constant.
Explicit Euler, 1-min internal step, 5-min observations.

```
dG/dt  = -(SG + X)·G + (EGP/1.6)·f_EGP(t) + Ra(t)/V_G - k_ex·e(t)·G - k_r·max(G - G_thr, 0)
dX/dt  = -p2·X + p2·SI·m_sleep·m_stress·m_ex(t)·I
dI/dt  = -n·I + beta·max(G - h, 0)·(1 + g_SU·s(t))·m_DPP4 + beta·k_SU·s(t) + I_exo(t)/V_I
dD1/dt = -D1/tau + CHO_in(t);   dD2/dt = (D1 - D2)/tau;   Ra = f_bio·D2/tau
dGi/dt = (G - Gi)/tau_i
f_EGP  = (1 + dawn·exp(-½((hour-6)/1.5)²)) · (1 - e_met·a_met(t)) · (1 + e_s·stress(t))
tau    = tau_meal·tau_s,  tau_meal = 40·(70/GI)^0.6·(1 + 0.006·fat + 0.003·protein + 0.012·fibre) min
```

* `s(t)`: sulfonylurea effect (normalised Bateman curve, amplitude (dose/ref)^0.6; glimepiride
  t½ 7 h, gliclazide MR 14 h, gliclazide 10 h); `I_exo`: glargine two-step depot (bioavailability 0.8,
  V_I = 120 mL/kg); `a_met`, DPP-4 and SGLT2 traces = exponentially weighted adherence from logged
  doses; SGLT2 lowers the renal threshold 180 → 105 mg/dL.
* `e(t)` = exercise intensity from 5-min steps, `m_ex = 1 + 0.5·trace` (τ 240 min);
  `m_sleep = 1 - 0.2·clip((6.5 - h_sleep)/2.5, 0, 1)` from the wearable's last night — informed by
  sleep-restriction studies reporting roughly 15–25 % lower insulin sensitivity after one short night;
  `stress` from HR above a personal 7-day baseline and HRV below baseline.
* Fixed constants: V_G 1.6 dL/kg, p2 0.025 /min, n 0.14 /min, τ_i 9 min, k_ex 0.0045, f_bio 0.9,
  k_SU 25, g_SU 0.6, e_met 0.15, e_s 0.10, h 105 mg/dL, k_r 0.0089·eGFR/100 /min. Values are chosen
  inside ranges reported for the Bergman minimal model and its extensions (SG ~10⁻², SI ~10⁻⁴ per
  µU/mL in T2D, p2 ~10⁻²) and the Dalla Man-style two-compartment gut; they are engineering choices,
  not fitted to clinical data.

Personalised parameters θ (EnKF, log / logit space with bounds):

| key | meaning | prior (from EHR) | bounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| SI | insulin sensitivity | 2.0e-4 · exp(-0.9(TyG-9.2) - 2.0(WHtR-0.57) - 0.02(BMI-25)), sd 0.5 | 2e-5 – 2e-3 |
| SG | glucose effectiveness | 0.009, sd 0.3 | 0.003 – 0.04 |
| beta | beta-cell responsiveness | 0.05 · exp(-0.025(dur-7) - 0.12(HbA1c-7.8) - 0.05·TCF7L2_T) · (C-pep/1.8)^0.6, sd 0.5 (0.35 with C-peptide) | 0.003 – 0.5 |
| EGP | basal endogenous glucose output (mg/kg/min, model units) | solved so fasting steady state = 0.85·FPG + 0.15·(ADAG eAG - 20), sd 0.25 | 0.8 – 5.0 |
| meal_rate | gut absorption speed (1/tau_s) | 1.0, sd 0.25 | 0.5 – 2.0 |
| dawn | dawn EGP amplitude | 0.15, sd 0.8 (logit) | 0 – 0.6 |

TyG = ln(TG × FPG / 2); ADAG eAG = 28.7 × HbA1c − 46.7. The twin never reads the truth archetype.

## 3. Data assimilation

Stochastic EnKF, 64 members, augmented state (7 dynamic states + 6 parameters). Every 5 min: propagate
(5 Euler steps), add process noise (G 2.5 mg/dL, X 2e-4, I 1.0) and a parameter random walk
(SI 0.012, SG 0.004, beta 0.008, EGP 0.006, tau 0.006, dawn 0.02 per step); update with the CGM
reading (observation sd = 5 + 6 % of the reading) using perturbed observations. Missing CGM → no update.
Innovation gating at 4σ rejects compression artefacts; after 3 consecutive rejections the update is
forced with 4× observation variance (prevents divergence on genuine fast changes). Multiplicative
inflation 1.001 on parameter anomalies; bounds by clipping in transformed space. The filter runs in
6-hour blocks with deterministic per-block seeds and float32 snapshots, so any past ensemble can be
reconstructed exactly (used by the API).

Settings were chosen on **validation** patients of a separate small run (twin-vs-truth MARD and
60/120-min forecast RMSE): inflation 1.01 → 1.001 improved 60-min RMSE from 35.6 to 33.6 mg/dL and
stabilised parameter trajectories; larger observation noise and smaller process noise did not help.

Forecasts propagate the ensemble with planned (prescribed) doses, decaying activity/stress traces and a
**habitual-meal prior** learned from the last 7 days of logged meals (per slot: frequency, timing,
carbohydrate; each member samples whether/when the meal happens). The p10–p90 interval is rescaled per
horizon so it covers 80 % of truth on validation patients (scale factors in `metrics.json →
forecast.interval_scale`).

## 4. Risk layer

* Decision points every 15 min from day 1; bedtime decision = 15 min after the wearable detects
  sustained sleep after 21:00 (≤ 23:45).
* Labels on **truth plasma glucose**: `hyper_2h` (>250 for ≥15 min starting within 120 min, currently
  <250), `hypo_1h` (<70 for ≥15 min starting within 60 min, currently ≥70), `nocturnal_hypo` (episode
  starting 00:00–06:00). A CGM-labelled variant is reported too.
* ~100 strictly causal features: CGM level/slopes/acceleration/variability, carbs-on-board and GL from
  logged meals, time since meal/dose, sulfonylurea/insulin/metformin effect traces, steps, HR/HRV vs
  personal baseline, stress, last-night sleep and 3-day debt, time of day, EHR statics, and **33 twin
  features** (ensemble forecast quantiles at +30/+60/+120, exceedance probabilities, posterior
  parameters and 24-h changes, insulin action, gut glucose, innovation/mismatch); plus 4 overnight twin
  features for the nocturnal task. A unit test verifies that perturbing any data after `t` leaves
  features at `t` unchanged.
* LightGBM (binary, scale_pos_weight = sqrt(neg/pos) ≤ 10, early stopping on validation AUPRC),
  isotonic calibration on validation patients, TreeSHAP (`pred_contrib`) drivers.
* Alerts: fire when p ≥ threshold, alert armed, cool-down elapsed (120 / 60 min) and CGM not already
  beyond the event threshold; re-arm when p < 0.7 × threshold or after 2 × cool-down. Threshold = lowest value keeping
  validation false alerts ≤ 1 per patient-day (nocturnal: ≤ 1 per patient-week, since there is one
  decision per night).
* Split: patient-level 70/15/15 stratified by (hidden) archetype; test patients are used only by
  `evaluate`.

## 5. Results (held-out synthetic test patients)

Dataset: 240 patients x 28 days (1,913,090 CGM points); train/val/test = 168/36/36 patients;
620,160 decision points and 6,240 bedtime decisions. Hidden archetype counts SIDD 65, SIRD 33, MARD 46,
IROD 96. Test-set figures (36 patients); `ci95` = patient-level bootstrap.

CGM-labelled variant (same predictions scored against CGM-derived labels): hyper_2h AUROC 0.936,
hypo_1h 0.928, nocturnal 0.802 — lower for hypoglycaemia because CGM noise and compression artefacts
create "events" that are not in the truth.

|---|---|---|---|
| CGM linear extrapolation | 0.611 / 0.186 | 0.944 / 0.055 | 0.809 / 0.041 |
| ML without twin features | 0.924 / 0.672 | 0.980 / 0.298 | 0.928 / 0.125 |

The hybrid is best on every task; the twin features add +0.013 AUROC / +0.037 AUPRC for hyper_2h and
+0.026 AUPRC for hypo_1h over the same ML without twin features. Twin-only probabilities are useful for
spikes but weak for hypoglycaemia on their own. Nocturnal results rest on 8 positive test nights.

**Forecast (twin ensemble median vs truth plasma glucose, test patients):**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 min | 29.8 | 11.9 % | 0.81 | 93.0 / 6.7 / 0.4 / 0.0 / 0.0 | 41.9 | 36.3 |

Raw ensemble intervals were too narrow (coverage 0.36–0.43); widening them by validation-fitted factors
3.10 / 2.99 / 2.78 gives ~0.81 coverage on test. Sync quality: the filtered twin's plasma-glucose estimate
has MARD 9.4 % vs truth, *slightly worse* than raw CGM (8.5 %), and its widened band covers truth 59 % of the
time — the EnKF cannot remove correlated sensor calibration drift, so we do not claim the twin denoises
CGM; its value is in forecasting and in the features it feeds the risk models.

Top TreeSHAP features: hyper_2h — twin P(>250 within 2 h), time of day, twin +2 h p90 forecast, time since
waking, recent steps; nocturnal — twin overnight mean forecast, basal-insulin action, time since SU dose,
24-h CV.

**What changed during development (validation data only):** (1) EnKF multiplicative inflation
1.01 → 1.001 (better 60/120-min forecasts, stable parameters); (2) forecast intervals rescaled to 80 %
validation coverage; (3) the alert hysteresis originally re-armed only when risk fell below 0.7 × threshold —
on validation, 96 % of hypo events had a supra-threshold probability in the hour before onset yet
alerts went silent for SU users whose risk stayed elevated; alerts now also re-arm after 2 × cool-down,
raising hypo_1h event sensitivity from 0.14 to 0.42 at 0.66 alerts/patient-day (thresholds re-selected on
validation; test patients were not used for any choice).

**Runtime** (12-core Windows laptop, 6 workers, full profile): generate 32 s; assimilate 20.4 min
(EnKF + ~2,700 ensemble forecasts per patient; measured while the test suite was also running); train 6.5
min (datasets 72 s + 6 LightGBM models); evaluate 4.2 min (metrics, alert ledger, validation, cached cohort
view, figures); export-demo 75 s. Total ≈ 33 min, so the default N = 240 x 28 days was kept.

## 5b. External validation on real data (REAL, `reports/real_validation.json`)

The unchanged twin (EHR priors, filter settings fixed on synthetic validation patients) was run on 90 open,
de-identified CGM records: CGMacros (45 people: healthy, prediabetes and T2D; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) and the
non-insulin records of ShanghaiT2DM (45 records, 42 people; CC BY 4.0). Nothing was tuned on these records.
Mappings are in [REAL_DATA.md](REAL_DATA.md).

| horizon | twin RMSE | twin MARD | twin 80 % coverage | twin Parkes A | persistence | linear | GlycoTwin LightGBM (OOF) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 min | 27.3 | 13.6 % | 0.7818 | 88.78 % | 18.52 | 23.23 | 14.06 |
| 60 min | 30.84 | 15.39 % | 0.7636 | 84.6 % | 28.83 | 45.96 | 21.7 |
| 120 min | 34.51 | 17.22 % | 0.7586 | 80.25 % | 40.6 | 90.61 | 27.87 |

All methods are scored on the same 89,977 timestamps.

| event (real CGM labels) | base rate | windows | GlycoTwin features AUROC / AUPRC | + twin features | Pratibimb synthetic model, no retraining |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥180 within 2 h | 0.1304 | 78,970 | 0.8681 / 0.5341 | 0.8703 / 0.5366 | 0.6628 / 0.2363 |
| >250 within 2 h | 0.0246 | 88,585 | 0.9232 / 0.3552 | 0.9235 / 0.3554 | 0.7902 / 0.1543 |

The models use GlycoTwin's patient-level 5-fold CV and fixed LightGBM settings. The 95 % person-level bootstrap
interval for the AUROC gain from twin features is −0.0018 to +0.0071 (≥180) and −0.0078 to +0.0102 (>250).

Reading: on real data the twin beats linear extrapolation at every horizon and persistence at 2 h, and its
synthetic-fitted intervals still cover 76–78 %. It is worse than persistence at 30–60 min and worse than a
data-driven LightGBM forecaster at every horizon. Its twin features add no measurable discrimination on real
data, and the synthetic-trained risk model transfers poorly. The synthetic cohort is 2.479× (≥180) and
5.439× (>250) more event-prone than the real records, and 1.532× more volatile over 30 min
(`synthetic_vs_real`).

## 6. Limitations

* Trained on synthetic data only; the truth simulator encodes our assumptions. Good synthetic scores show
  the method works *if the world resembles the simulator*. The real-data check (5b) shows that the synthetic
  accuracy and the twin-feature gain do not carry over to real CGM, and that the synthetic cohort is too
  hyperglycaemic and too volatile. Prospective clinical validation is required before any claim.
* The truth simulator shares the minimal-model family with the twin (it is richer, not different in
  kind); real physiology will be further from the twin than our test patients are.
* Parameter estimates are not identifiable in a strict sense — the twin compensates for structural
  mismatch (e.g. no hepatic insulin compartment), so posterior SI/beta are "effective" values; use them
  as trends, not lab measurements.
* Meal logging is assumed timely; unlogged snacks are only caught indirectly (innovation features).
* Nocturnal hypoglycaemia is rare in the cohort, so its metrics have wide confidence intervals.
* Food composition/GI values are approximate (see DATA_CARD); some are estimates.
* Alerts and recommendations are illustrative; thresholds were tuned for a demo trade-off, not safety.
